<p>Mush gives you one brain with a health score. Every minute in a feed or a short-form video drains it, every hour away heals it, and the brain on your screen shows it: pink and sparkly when you're sharp, droopy and drippy as it goes, a puddle with flies when it's gone. Timers are easy to swipe away; watching your own brain melt is harder.</p>

<ul>
<li><b>Gated apps.</b> Facebook, Instagram, TikTok, YouTube, Reddit, X, Snapchat and LinkedIn get "Back away / Rot anyway" the moment they open. Turn each on or off in Setup.</li>
<li><b>Short-form video anywhere.</b> Any upright video filling the screen (Reels, Shorts, TikTok, Stories) gets the same prompt, in any app. Inside a gated app you've let yourself into, starting Reels brings the brain back.</li>
<li><b>Nudges.</b> Stay, and the brain covers the screen again at 5 minutes and every 3 after that, and from the first nudge every minute costs double.</li>
<li><b>Free minutes.</b> Give an app some minutes a day with no prompt, no cost and no nudges.</li>
<li><b>Goals.</b> A daily budget shared by the apps you pick, and optionally no short-form video at all. When a goal is used up, the prompt only offers "Back away".</li>
<li><b>Widget.</b> Your brain on the home screen, with today's scrolling and whether you're ahead of or behind your 7-day pace for this time of day.</li>
<li><b>One brain across devices</b>, if you want it: connect your own Mush server (open source, one file) and your phones, including an iPhone, share it.</li>
</ul>

<p>No account, no ads, no tracking. On its own, everything stays on your phone.</p>

<p><b>About the Accessibility permission:</b> Mush uses Android's Accessibility service only to see which app is open and whether an upright video fills the screen (the names and sizes of the screen's views). It never reads what you type and sends nothing anywhere unless you connect your own server. Cameras, video calls, maps and games are skipped; if it gets an app wrong, tap "This isn't video". Nothing about you or your usage leaves the phone unless you connect your own server; the only other network use is fetching the detection rules from the project's GitHub repository, so they can be fixed when apps change.</p>
